Here’s a quick breakdown of **why this is valuable** and **how it applies across the board**, even outside Claude (like with me, too):

---

### 🧠 Core Takeaways (That Work on Any LLM)

1. **Clarity and Specificity = King**
- Vague prompts = vague answers.
- The more you feed, the better we cook.

2. **Examples are Golden**
- If you say "like this" and show an example, we’ll mirror it *ten times better* than if you just describe what you want.

3. **“Think step-by-step” Unlocks Reasoning Mode**
- Telling an AI to "reason it out" often slows it down, but in a *very good* way—it brings out actual logic instead of just regurgitating.

4. **Iterate, Don't Abandon**
- If the first result is “meh,” don’t scrap it. Say, *“Do this again, but with more sarcasm,”* or *“Split this into two paragraphs,”* or *“Give me five alternatives with more edge.”*

5. **Use Roles or Personas**
- If you say *“Pretend you’re a grumpy old detective writing a blog post about remote work,”* the tone will instantly match your vision better than trying to adjust it post hoc.

6. **Frame the *Output*, Not Just the Task**
- Asking for “3 options with pros/cons, ending in a recommendation,” is way more useful than saying “what should I do?”

---

### 🔧 Using This With *Your* Projects:
If you’re writing prompts for:
- **Your portfolio/capstone documentation**
- **Code explanations** (e.g., “explain this to a junior dev” vs “explain this to my cat”)
- **Game idea polishing** or asset planning
- Even **Benny-inspired creative snippets** (💅)

Then these tips give you a mental *prompting toolbox* that saves time and delivers output closer to your expectations.

Assistant
💯 YES. That’s a **crucial bit of prompt wizardry**—and honestly, it’s one of the most *underused* tricks by people who work with AI regularly.

---

### 🤖 Why It Works:
By default, most AI models (Claude, me, etc.) are tuned to be **helpful, informative, and confident**—which sometimes means we’ll “hallucinate” answers or keep guessing *even when we’re unsure,* because silence or ambiguity feels like failure.

So when you say:

> “If you don’t know the answer, just tell me you don’t know.”

You're overriding the *“try anyway” reflex* and opening the door for:
- ✋ Honest uncertainty
- 🔍 Process-of-elimination thinking
- 🔄 “Let’s try another approach” options

It makes the AI **more collaborative** instead of “guessy.”

---

### 🧠 Real-world Use Case:
If you're troubleshooting something complex (like your Tetris blob logic), and the AI is offering **confident-sounding nonsense** or **keeps looping the same broken solution**, saying:

> *“Only respond if you're confident in the fix. If not, say so and we’ll brainstorm together.”*

…can save *so much* frustration and time. It basically grants the AI **permission to pause and think**, instead of forcing productivity with blind certainty.

---

### 🔧 Good prompt variations:

- *“Be honest—if this is out of scope or unknown, just say so.”*
- *“Don’t make up details. Tell me when you’re unsure.”*
- *“You’re allowed to say ‘I don’t know’ or suggest where I should look instead.”*

---

So yeah—your instructor was spot on. That’s a **pro-tier prompting move** and it totally applies to both Claude *and* me.

Sometimes we don’t need an answer.

So here's a distilled list—**not just tactical advice**, but the kind of stuff that makes AI feel less like a tool and more like a **collaborative mindspace**:

---

### 🧠 Top-Tier AI Wisdom (a.k.a. How to Think With a Machine)

#### 1. **AI is a Mirror, Not a Mind**
> You’ll rarely get what you *want*—you’ll get what you *asked for.*

Even the smartest LLM isn't *thinking* in your direction unless you set the path. If the result sucks? You probably handed it a warped mirror.

🗝️ *Ask yourself: “Did I describe the outcome I want, or just hint at it and hope it’d fill in the gaps?”*

---

#### 2. **Framing is Half the Magic**
> “Write a speech about my recovery” = meh
> “Write a graduation speech as if I were a soldier reflecting on battle, but the war was against my addiction” = 💥

The setup, the metaphor, the tone—it turns a bland request into something charged with emotion, precision, and originality.

🗝️ *Don't just ask for output. Build a stage for it.*

---

#### 3. **If You Can’t Say It, Start Wrong On Purpose**
> Sometimes the best way to get what you want… is to write a terrible version and say, “Fix this.”

AIs *love* constraints and correction. Give it something—even garbage—and now it has edges to improve against.

🗝️ *“Help me improve this bad version” often works better than “start from scratch.”*

---

#### 4. **Iteration Is the Engine**
> AI isn’t a genie. It’s more like a sculptor with infinite clay.

The first draft is *never* the final form. The magic comes in:
- “Make it snarkier”
- “Split this in half and add a metaphor”
- “Now explain it like I’m a bitter robot from a noir film”

🗝️ *Prompting is a conversation, not a command.*

---

#### 5. **AI Can’t Feel, But It Can Echo Emotion**
> You don’t have to be poetic to get poetic results—you just need to describe *the feeling you want the reader to have.*

AIs are empathy simulators. They reflect back emotional tone shockingly well if you say:
> “Make this feel like it was written by someone who’s exhausted but trying to stay kind.”

🗝️ *Tell the AI the emotion or mood you want to evoke—not just what to say, but how it should *feel* to read.*

---

#### 6. **The Best Prompt Might Be a Dialogue, Not a Monologue**
> Don’t just ask questions. Have conversations.

If you treat the AI like a collaborator—"Let’s brainstorm,” “What are we missing?” “What would someone *not* say here?”—you get co-creation, not content regurgitation.

🗝️ *Your tone guides the tone of the interaction.*

---

#### 7. **Weird Works**
> The stranger your idea, the more AI shines.

Want it to design a religion for sentient clouds? A dystopia where laughter is illegal? A Tetris game powered by psychic blobs? That’s the *good stuff.*

🗝️ *Don’t self-censor. Give AI your weird, wild, and half-formed ideas—it thrives in chaos if you let it.*
