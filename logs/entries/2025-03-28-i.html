Assistant
Yeah, this does sound like it could be related to the GLP-1 injections (like semaglutide, Wegovy, Ozempic, etc.). Those meds can definitely change your appetite—but the effect isn't always *just* appetite suppression. Here's a breakdown of what's likely going on:

### 🧠 **Why your appetite feels weird**
1. **Delayed gastric emptying (slower digestion):**
GLP-1 meds slow down how fast your stomach empties. That means even a normal-sized meal can make you feel *really* full, and for longer than usual. That explains the discomfort Friday after Slim Chickens.

2. **Appetite suppression… inconsistently:**
You might *not* feel super hungry at normal times, then suddenly it hits hard—like your body’s catching up. So you’re not necessarily overeating on purpose, you’re just trying to compensate for that hunger signal hitting late.

3. **Cravings vs. hunger confusion:**
High-carb or sugary stuff (like cake) might suddenly seem *super satisfying* because your body is hunting for quick energy—but the fullness catches up faster, and satiety feels off-balance.

4. **Sleep rhythm and hunger:**
If your sleep is fragmented or short (which you’ve mentioned before), that messes with ghrelin and leptin—hunger and satiety hormones. That can lead to weird swings in appetite, especially if your meals are misaligned with your waking hours.

### 🥩 **Why you’re still hungry after the pork loins**
Even though they’re solid protein, they’re *lean*—not much fat. Fat helps signal long-term satiety. If you didn’t have many carbs with it either (like rice, veggies, or toast), your body might still be signaling for “more.”

### 😵‍💫 **So what can you try?**
- **Pair protein with a little fat or fiber**: e.g., a small veggie side, some roasted carrots, or a few crackers with hummus or cheese. Even a half-avocado helps.
- **Watch for “late” hunger**: If you're hungry out of nowhere, it might be a delayed response. A small snack earlier might’ve prevented it.
- **Plan light but *dense* meals**: GLP-1 slows digestion, so aim for meals that are satisfying but not heavy—like a wrap instead of a giant plate.

You’re not doing anything wrong—it’s just that GLP-1 shifts how your body signals hunger/fullness. If this pattern keeps frustrating you, you can also bring it up at your next VTC check-in or with whoever’s monitoring your treatment. They might help tweak your strategy around it.

Yup, that’s *exactly* what it sounds like—classic “balloon burger” syndrome from loosely packed patties!

When ground beef isn’t packed tight enough, or the shape is a little too tall in the center, the outer parts cook and tighten faster, which squeezes the inside upward, causing that puffed-up look. It’s super common with hand-patted burgers if they’re:

- Too thick in the middle
- Not packed tightly enough
- Or don’t have any kind of center indentation

**Pro Tip for next time:**
After shaping your patties, gently press a little divot into the center of each one with your thumb. Doesn’t have to be deep—just enough to flatten the top a bit. This helps them cook more evenly and stay burger-shaped instead of turning into meat domes.
