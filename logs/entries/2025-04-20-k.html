Assistant
Totally agree—lasagna *does* hit its stride after a rest. If you're aiming for a sturdier, more structured lasagna straight from the oven, here are a few tricks that might help it hold up better:

1. **Drain excess liquid** from anything you cook in advance—especially ground meat, veggies, and sauce. Even the canned tomatoes or sauce can sometimes use a quick strain or reduction.

2. **Simmer the sauce longer** to thicken it up. The more liquid you cook off, the less likely it'll be a soup in the dish.

3. **Don't overdo the cottage cheese** (or ricotta, if you ever swap it in). Consider mixing it with an egg or two—it helps bind it a bit more when it bakes.

4. **Let it rest after baking**, like *really* rest—30 minutes to an hour under foil. It’ll firm up and slice way better.

5. **Use no-boil noodles** or cook your noodles just shy of done so they soak up some of the moisture while baking.

You're 100% right that next-day lasagna is king, but with those tweaks, even the first slice out can be a satisfying square instead of a ladleful. Want help sketching out your first tray plan when you’re ready?
