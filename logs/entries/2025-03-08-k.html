Great question—and also super cool that you got to work on EV battery chassis assembly!

So the phrase is actually **"tool and die"**, not *dye*. Easy mix-up because they sound the same, but the meanings are way different.

### 🔧 What’s “Tool and Die”?
“Tool and die” work refers to **custom tooling and precision metalworking**, especially involving machines that cut, shape, or form metal. A **die** (pronounced "dye") is a specialized tool used to cut or shape material—kind of like a mold or stamp.

In the context of stripped thread holes, though, what you're probably thinking of is **"tapping" or "re-tapping" threads**, sometimes using **thread repair kits** like:
- **Helicoils** (insertable coils to restore stripped threads)
- **Thread chasers** or **taps/dies** to re-cut existing threads

So even though “tool and die” is its own metalworking field, when your team was fixing threads, they were likely using **a tap (tool)** or **a die (tool)** to restore the thread shape. That’s probably why the phrase “tool and die” came up—it’s the name of the tools *and* the trade.

### ⚙️ Quick rundown of tap vs. die:
- **Tap**: Cuts threads **inside** a hole (like fixing a stripped bolt hole)
- **Die**: Cuts threads **on the outside** of a rod (like making a new bolt)

So yeah—it’s not “tool and dye” like color dye. It’s “tool and die” like the tools that make stuff die-cut or form-fitted. Pretty metal 🤘

Let me know if you wanna see examples of taps or Helicoils and how they work.

Assistant
You’re right on the money with that instinct—there *are* a few ways to go about it depending on how deep you want to dive and how much structure the site gives you.

Here’s a breakdown of your options:

### 🔧 **Option 1: Site Downloader Approach (like HTTrack or wget)**
- **Pros**: Super easy to just grab the site locally.
- **Cons**: You get raw HTML without parsing logic—good for static mirrors but not ideal for *targeted data extraction* (like loot names, min level, slots, bonuses, etc.).
- Might work for some very small manual parsing, but gets messy fast.

### 🧹 **Option 2: Web Scraper Tools (like BeautifulSoup in Python or Cheerio in Node.js)**
- **Best for**: Targeted data pulls from structured HTML like tables, lists, or infoboxes (which DDOwiki uses a lot).
- **How**: You’d fetch a loot page, parse the HTML, and extract just what you want.

If you go this route:
- **Python + BeautifulSoup** = dead simple, well-documented
- **Node.js + Cheerio** = good if you want to stick closer to the web dev/React ecosystem

### 🛠️ **Option 3: Use Their API (If They Have One)**
- DDOwiki doesn’t advertise a traditional API, but they *are* built on MediaWiki, which has a **MediaWiki API** that can return page data in structured formats like JSON or XML.
- That might give you access to specific pages or even the raw templates.

👉 Example endpoint:
```
https://ddowiki.com/api.php?action=parse&page=Example_Item_Name&format=json
```

### 🧠 Ideal Strategy:
1. **Use MediaWiki API** to get the raw page data.
2. **Parse templates or infoboxes** from that data (if consistent).
3. **Fallback** to scraping with a parser like BeautifulSoup/Cheerio for trickier cases.
4. **Store** the parsed info in a local JSON or CSV to use in your loot builder.
