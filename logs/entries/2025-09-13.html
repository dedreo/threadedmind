That AskReddit thread is touching on a legitimate (if currently niche) concept in infosec and counter-surveillance circles: **data poisoning** or **data flooding** as an *active obfuscation tactic*.

Let’s break it down:

---

### 🧠 **The Idea**: Obfuscation by Data Saturation

Rather than locking down data (which is hard), the concept is to **intentionally flood the system with *so much false or contradictory data*** that any surveillance dataset becomes **noisy, expensive to parse, and unreliable**.

Examples:
- Posting multiple conflicting location check-ins
- Using bots to populate social feeds with plausible but false life events
- Feeding AI systems (e.g. ad trackers, sentiment analysis, etc.) false preferences
- Running fake Google searches or queries to confuse profiling

This is a variation of **security through disinformation** rather than traditional **privacy**.

---

### ✅ **Why it *could* work** (to a point):
- **Automated systems** (ad engines, behavior classifiers, OSINT scrapers) struggle with **truth extraction** in noisy environments.
- Metadata poisoning is already used by some activists and privacy tools (e.g., **TrackMeNot** sends fake Google queries).
- In intelligence analysis, there's a concept called **"chaff"**: burying truth under a mountain of falsehoods. If you can't hide the signal, drown it.

---

### ❌ **Why it’s *not enough* on its own**:
1. **Historic Data Is Immutable**
Like Humble_Ladder said, once they’ve got *pre-flood* data, they can always filter by date. Past behavior is often more telling than present.

2. **Behavioral Patterns Are Hard to Fake**
Bots can't easily simulate **natural human entropy**—your scroll behavior, typo patterns, timing rhythms, all get fingerprinted and tracked beyond just content.

3. **Correlated Data from Multiple Sources**
Surveillance doesn’t rely on one input. Your phone GPS, browser telemetry, and social logins still paint a strong triangulated profile.

4. **Counterflooding ≠ Anonymity**
You might pollute the pool, but you're still swimming in it. Your devices, logins, cookies, hardware IDs… all leak identity in ways that floods won’t mask.

---

### 🛠️ **Where It *Does* Have Value**:
- **Activists/journalists in hostile regimes** sometimes use these tactics to **hide real ops in plain sight**.
- **Online personas** can use flood-based disinfo to maintain **layered identities** (e.g. sockpuppet accounts with conflicting traits).
- It’s a **cool layer** in an **onion-model** privacy strategy: flood + encrypt + rotate devices + mixnet routing (Tor, I2P, etc).

---

### 🧬 Philosophically:

> _“If you cannot be private, be illegible.”_ — A growing theme in post-privacy thought.

Flooding is about breaking the **utility** of data rather than its existence. If AI can't trust the data, it can't weaponize it.
