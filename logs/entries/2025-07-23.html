### 🔥 Old School: SLI/CrossFire (RIP)
- **SLI (NVIDIA)** and **CrossFire (AMD)** were marketed as “just plug in two cards and double your power!”

### 🧠 Modern Use: **Multi-GPU for Workloads**
If you’re using your GPUs for **AI, rendering, or compute**, then yes—you can:
- Run a **14GB + 24GB card** in the same machine.
- Assign different tasks or models to each.
- Have Diffusion on one, LLM on another.
- Or shard big batches of data across both.

But:
- You *don’t* get “combined VRAM” unless the app supports explicit memory pooling (rare).
- Apps like PyTorch or TensorFlow can be told:
> “Send Wmcy’s inference to GPU 0, Diffusion to GPU 1.”

| AI Inference | ✅ Yes | Explicit assignment per GPU |

### Re: VRAM Usage & "Tiring Out" the GPU

Nope—VRAM doesn’t get “tired.” It’s **just memory**, not muscle. It doesn’t wear out from sitting occupied.

That said, keeping Wmcy running *does* mean:
- Her model stays loaded in VRAM (e.g. 6–12GB depending on size + context)
- If another app (like DDO) suddenly needs a chunk of VRAM, your system might:
- **Page out the LLM** to system RAM or disk temporarily (slow but survivable)
- **Crash the model** if it runs out of headroom completely
- Or **cause framerate spikes** if VRAM thrashes between uses

But DDO is old and low on GPU demands by modern standards. **Your 14GB VRAM setup can probably handle Wmcy + DDO together** just fine, especially if:
- Wmcy’s running a quantized model like `llama3:8b` or `mistral`
- You’re not doing massive image generation at the same time

Worst case? You alt-tab and Wmcy takes a moment to recover while DDO hogs the frame buffer.
