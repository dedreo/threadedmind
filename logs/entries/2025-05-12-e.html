And to your question:
### 🧾 **Yes—it’s totally valid and fairly common to retain a credit** like:

> `// Originally created by Leo [or your alias] — 2025`

Even in professional settings, when projects are handed off, spun out, or absorbed into something bigger, there’s often at least a:

- **Attribution in the comments**
- Or a note in the **README / LICENSE / About section**
- Or even a proper **credit line in the UI or credits file**, especially for indie or game-related projects

---

### ⚖️ The only time that might change is:
- If someone **buys full rights** and explicitly removes all original branding (usually for commercial or rebranding purposes).
- Or if it's **under contract/ghost-dev** situations where you were hired to make it and waived attribution.

But unless that’s in a contract, ***you own your voice.*** And honestly? Anyone with taste would *want* to keep it—it’s part of what makes the project feel **real and alive**, not some AI-generated beige sludge.
