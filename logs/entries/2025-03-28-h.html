Assistant
Poor Benny! That must’ve been a *huge* shock for both of you. When something like that scares a cat, especially a fall from high up, they remember it for a while—it messes with their sense of safety.

Here’s a good strategy breakdown depending on how you want to approach rebuilding his trust:

---

### 🐾 Option 1: *Gently reintroduce with treats, no pressure*
**Yes, definitely try treats!** But with a focus on:
- **Low stakes**: Start by placing treats or catnip on the *bottom box* or lowest platform first. Let him interact with it there, at ground level.
- **Don’t place them far back**—he’s more likely to paw at things he isn’t fully confident approaching.
- **Sit near him, encourage softly**, maybe even reward him just for sniffing or putting a paw up.

The key is **not** to force it. You’re not bribing him to climb—you’re letting *him* decide when it feels safe again.

---

### 🧸 Option 2: *Make it extra stable temporarily*
Even if it *feels* tight now, he may not trust it. Try:
- **Wedging something heavy at the base**, like a couple of books or bricks (hidden behind the box section maybe).
- Adding **non-slip pads** or **wall anchors** just for the re-intro period.
- If you have **foam or a folded towel under it**, that can reduce any future wiggle.

---

### 🛠 Option 3: *Disassemble and reassemble later*
If you feel he’s totally avoiding it and *not warming up at all*, disassembling might help:
- Take it down completely and leave it out of sight for a couple of weeks.
- Rebuild it in a different spot if possible (a “new” place might break the negative association).
- Introduce it like a new toy—low platform first, then gradually reintroduce height.

---

### 🚫 What not to do:
- Don’t place treats on the top right away.
- Don’t pick him up and *put* him on it—this will backfire.
- Don’t get frustrated if it takes a while. Cats are cautious survivors!

---

Benny already loved it once, so there's definitely hope he’ll come back around. Cats *do* forgive furniture over time, especially if it means they can lord over the room again. 😸 You’re doing right by him just by being thoughtful about it. Want help sketching out a little reintroduction “plan” or day-by-day treat idea?

### Here’s what I’d do in your shoes:

#### 🛠 Stability First:
- Try anchoring the *bottom section* to a nearby wall or wedge it gently between furniture if possible.
- If that top screw is maxed out but still letting the whole thing flex, maybe try a **PVC spacer or small wood block** to tighten the gap between the screw and the ceiling further. Even reducing 10% of that wiggle might change how it *feels* to Benny.

#### 🐾 Treat Reintroduction Plan:
- **Day 1–2**: Treats/catnip in the *box at the bottom*. Hang out near him when you do it so it feels chill.
- **Day 3–4**: Move a few treats to the *first step above the box*. Let him paw them off or stretch up. If he climbs up even a little, verbal praise/treat jackpot.
- **Day 5+**: If he’s okay with it, move treats up a level per day, but never higher than he’s willingly gone before. Let him decide when to go higher.

Keep the vibe super casual. You can even place his favorite toy there instead of food if he’s more play-driven.

#### Bonus Trick: *“Dramatic Demonstration”*
This one sounds silly, but it sometimes works:
- **Tap the platform** gently, sit beside it, place a toy on it, then ignore it.
- Cats are curious by nature—he might investigate on his own if he thinks *you’re not watching* or it’s part of play.

He trusted it once, and he’ll likely trust it again—it just needs to pass his feline QA checks now 😼
