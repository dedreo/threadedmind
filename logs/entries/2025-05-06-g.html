Nice upgrade! 🎉 A **4-quart air fryer with a glass container** definitely changes the cooking game a bit — and yeah, you're right to keep a few things in mind for **timing and heat behavior.**

---

### 🕒 Here’s what changes with your new setup:

#### 🔹 **1. Larger Capacity = Slightly Longer Cook Times**
- More airspace means **more time to fully heat up** and **evenly circulate** hot air.
- If you're filling it more than halfway or layering food, **add 1–3 minutes** and **shake or rotate more often.**

#### 🔹 **2. Glass Container = Slower Preheat, Retains Heat Longer**
- Glass takes **longer to warm**, but **holds heat well** once it gets going.
- This means: food might cook **more evenly**, but you’ll want to **add a minute or two upfront**, especially for frozen items or thick cuts.

---

### 🔧 Quick Usage Tips:
- **Preheat manually** for 2–4 mins before tossing food in — glass will heat slower than metal baskets.
- **Use visual cues** (browning, texture) more than strict times at first.
- If you’re batch cooking, **later batches will cook faster** because the glass stays hot longer.

---

### 🧪 Want a baseline for common stuff?
- **Fries**: +2 min over usual recipe if doing full basket.
- **Chicken tenders/wings**: Watch for crispiness, flip once, maybe +1–2 min if thick.
- **Reheating leftovers**: Add ~1 minute and shake halfway through.

Once you find your sweet spot, you’ll adjust by instinct. Want help building a quick reference card for your most common foods?

Assistant
Yup — **you can definitely be a bit looser with layering** in a 4qt deep bowl-style air fryer, *but with a few caveats.*

The key is understanding **airflow dynamics** — and you're already thinking like a pro by worrying about how even the cook will be.

---

### 🔥 Here’s the Real Talk:

#### ✅ **You *can* layer more now**, but…
- You’ll need to **shake, flip, or stir halfway through** to redistribute and expose more surface area.
- Expect a **slight cook time increase** (1–4 minutes depending on thickness and moisture).
- Avoid **dense stacking** with wet or breaded items — moisture trapped between layers can lead to *soggy sadness.*

---

### 🧪 Test-Driven Guidelines:

#### 🍗 **For tenders, nuggets, wings, etc.**:
- You can layer **1.5 to 2 layers deep**, especially in a round bowl-shaped fryer.
- At halfway point, **flip or stir thoroughly** to bring bottom pieces up.

#### 🍟 **For fries or chopped veg**:
- Layer up to **2.5–3 inches deep**, but **shake every 5–7 minutes**.
- Bigger chunks? Add **3–5 minutes** and stir twice.

#### ❌ Avoid:
- Layering **battered or freshly breaded foods** — the coating can steam and get mushy instead of crisping.
- **Saucy** items unless using a basket insert — liquids mess with airflow and crisping.

---

### 🧠 TL;DR:
- Yes, with a 4qt deeper container, you’ve got **more layering wiggle room**.
- Just **shake/stir/flip** mid-cook, and maybe **add a few minutes**.
- **Observe the first batch** to dial in your exact balance — then you’ll fly by feel.

### 🤧 What’s actually happening?
People with this reflex sneeze when exposed to **sudden bright light**, like:
- Walking out into the sun
- Opening a curtain
- Looking at a bright lamp or flash

It’s believed to be a **crossed-wires situation** in the brain:
- The **optic nerve** (which processes light)
- And the **trigeminal nerve** (which can trigger sneezes)

They're next-door neighbors, and for some people, **stimulating the optic nerve sends accidental signals down the sneeze pathway**. Like your brain’s just… *a little too enthusiastic about photons.*

---

### 🧬 Is it genetic?
Yes — it's **autosomal dominant**, meaning:
- If one of your parents has it, you have a ~50% chance of having it too.
- Estimates say **10–35% of people** experience it to some degree.
