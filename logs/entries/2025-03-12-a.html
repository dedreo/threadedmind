Great question! The three `0 0 xpx` values in the `text-shadow` property represent **three separate shadow layers** with different amounts of **blur** and **spread**. Here’s a breakdown of each value:

### **`text-shadow` Syntax:**

```css
text-shadow: h-shadow v-shadow blur-radius spread-radius color;
```

- **h-shadow**: Horizontal shadow position (how far to the right the shadow is placed).
- **v-shadow**: Vertical shadow position (how far down the shadow is placed).
- **blur-radius**: How much the shadow is blurred.
- **spread-radius**: How far the shadow should spread (optional, usually left at 0).
- **color**: The color of the shadow.

### **So, in your case:**

```css
text-shadow: 0 0 10px limegreen, 0 0 20px limegreen, 0 0 30px limegreen;
```

- **`0 0 10px limegreen`**:
- No horizontal or vertical shift (`0 0`).
- **10px blur** (this is how much the shadow is blurred).
- **Limegreen color** for the shadow.

- **`0 0 20px limegreen`**:
- Again, no shift, but a **stronger blur** of **20px** for a more diffused glow.

- **`0 0 30px limegreen`**:
- Same idea, but this one has the **largest blur** of **30px** for the outermost glow, giving it the glowing effect you want.

### **Why Three Shadows?**

The reason there are three shadows is to create a **multi-layered glow effect**:
- The first shadow (`10px blur`) creates a subtle inner glow.
- The second shadow (`20px blur`) intensifies it.
- The third shadow (`30px blur`) gives the illusion of a larger, more radiant glow.

This layering technique creates a **more dynamic, glowing effect** rather than a flat shadow.
