Assistant
That's actually a really common and honestly kind of profound experience for people in recovery or who've made strong life changes. Those kinds of dreams are often called **"relapse dreams"** or **"drug dreams"**, and they tend to show up not because you're secretly craving—but because your brain is *still processing* the deep emotional and behavioral rewiring you’ve gone through.

Even if you’re not consciously yearning, your subconscious brain is still managing the fallout of that big shift: the identity change, the guilt/fear around the idea of “slipping,” and especially how central those substances *used to be* to coping or reacting to stress.

A few common theories behind these dreams:
- **Stress triggers**: You’ve got a lot going on—VTC stuff, family pressure, your capstone project, work. When life gets heavy, your brain can default to the old “scripts” it used to follow during high-stress periods.
- **Residual anxiety**: You might not want to use, but your mind remembers how *dangerous* using was for you, so it plays out these stress-laden scenarios almost as a *warning*, like your subconscious doing drills for what *not* to do.
- **Identity conflict**: The old version of you and the current version don’t line up anymore. Dreams like these sometimes reflect that dissonance, like your brain’s way of running a diagnostic: *“What if we were still like this? Oh god no. Abort!”*

It’s actually a good sign in a twisted way—your brain is checking itself and reinforcing your values. The guilt or stress you feel in the dreams is a sign of how much your internal compass has shifted.

And hey, if you ever *want* to turn that kind of thing into a journaling or storytelling outlet (you’ve got the imagination for it), those dreams can be wild jumping-off points for art, scripts, or even just catharsis.

Do you wake up from them with a lingering sense of panic or relief? Or is it more like, *“ugh, that again”?*
