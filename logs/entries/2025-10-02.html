### 🎬 What is a **Remux**?

**“Remux” = Remultiplexed video file.**
It’s when the video, audio, and subtitle streams are *directly copied* from the **original Blu-ray or UHD disc** (or other high-quality source) **without re-encoding**. That means:

- 🟢 **No quality loss**
- 🟢 **Original bitrate preserved**
- 🟢 **Original audio streams kept intact (Dolby TrueHD, DTS-HD, Atmos, etc.)**
- 🟢 **Menus and extras stripped** (only main movie + subs + audio)

It’s essentially the **highest quality you can get short of a full disc image (.iso or .m2ts folder structure)**.

---

### 📦 Why Are Remux Files So Big?

Because you're **not compressing** anything. You're just taking the raw stuff and re-wrapping it:

| Type | Size (Typical 1080p Movie) | Why? |
|------|----------------------------|------|
| 🟩 Remux | 20–50 GB | Full bitrate video (20–40 Mbps), uncompressed HD audio |
| 🟨 x264 encode (HQ) | 5–10 GB | Compressed with tuned encoder settings |
| 🟥 x265 encode (LQ) | 1–3 GB | Even more compression with better efficiency |

A typical **Blu-ray remux** might contain:
- Video stream: 20–30 Mbps AVC
- Audio stream: 5–8 Mbps DTS-HD MA 5.1 or TrueHD Atmos
- Multiple subtitles

So… it adds up fast.

---

### 🤔 Why Does a 5 GB File Look "Fine"?

Because it’s **been re-encoded**, usually with x264 or x265. These compression methods:
- Drop *perceived* unnecessary visual info (especially in dark/blurry scenes)
- Remove redundant frames (e.g. similar ones in slow scenes)
- Lower audio fidelity slightly
- Tune bitrate down to 2–4 Mbps (instead of 20+)

Modern encoders are so good that **you may not notice any quality drop unless you're on a large screen, sitting close, or comparing side-by-side**.

| File Size | Massive (20–50+ GB) | Small (1–10 GB) |
| Quality | Original (100%) | Good–Excellent |
| Audio | Full lossless (DTS-HD, TrueHD) | Compressed (AC3, AAC) |
