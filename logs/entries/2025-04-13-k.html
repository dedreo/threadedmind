### 🌐 **2. Multiplayer and Saving Stuff**

Yup! For **live multiplayer** (especially with things like blob-triggered power-ups that affect opponents in real-time), you’ll need at least:

#### 🖧 A Server or Backend (eventually):
- **Matchmaking & Real-time syncing** (whose turn, where blocks are, which blobs are active)
- **Power-up communication** (e.g., “Player A used snowflake blob; Player B's next piece is messed up”)
- **Saving scores, replays, user stats** (so you can flex your wins or track progress)

#### Options:
- **Socket.io (with Node.js):** Great for real-time games with fast updates. Classic choice for React-based projects.
- **Firebase (Firestore + Realtime Database):** Simpler to start with, and handles auth and storage too.
- **Supabase:** An open-source Firebase alternative with SQL-like control.
- **Self-hosted server:** Using Express or something similar, if you want full control.
- **Game server frameworks:** If you shift to something like Godot later, it has built-in multiplayer tools too.

But you *don’t need multiplayer right away*. You can totally:
1. Build single player first.
2. Create local multiplayer (like old-school split screen).
3. Add online features once core mechanics are tight.

### 💥 More Power-Up Ideas

Here’s a few more power-up blob concepts that could mix things up:

- **Blue Blob** – Classic freeze: temporarily locks your opponent’s piece movement for 2–3 seconds (but still lets their piece fall). Forces them to *deal with it* fast.

- **Yellow Blob** – Swap: makes the opponent’s next piece the same as yours (could help or hinder them depending on the situation).

- **Red Blob** – Reverse controls for 5 seconds — classic “whaaaaat’s happening!?” sabotage.

- **Green Blob** – Garbage cleaner: removes your own lowest line with no penalty, useful if you're almost maxed out.

- **Purple Blob** – Ghost block: the next piece for your opponent *looks* like one shape, but locks in as a different one. Trickery!

---

### 🧊 Regarding Shapes: Single Block vs. Full Shape Sprites

It’s *absolutely* possible (and common!) to build shapes using a single block texture — especially in Tetris-inspired games.

#### ✅ Pros of Using a Single Ice Block:
- **Less asset work** — only one tile needed.
- **Dynamic color/effects** — can tint blocks based on shape or powerup.
- **Easy shape customization** — changing shapes is just about rearranging blocks.
- **Grid alignment** — keeps logic tidy for collision, gravity, rotation, etc.

#### 🚫 Full-Shape Sprites:
- More artistic freedom for *themed shapes* (like a “snowflake tetromino”), but...
- Harder to rotate dynamically.
- Requires more assets.
- Risk of visual confusion unless very distinct.

---

If you're going with the icy aesthetic, using a single ice block graphic with shader effects or subtle glow/color overlays (based on shape or powerup) gives you a super clean and readable look — like *Puyo Puyo Tetris*’s clarity but with your blob chaos layered in.

### ✅ **Free & Legal Sound/Music Resources**

#### 🎵 **Music:**
- [**Free Music Archive**](https://freemusicarchive.org/) – Broad and searchable, great for lo-fi or quirky tracks.
