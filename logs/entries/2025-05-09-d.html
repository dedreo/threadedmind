### 🎧 **Using Sound Effects – Legal Breakdown:**

#### ✅ **Safe Options**
- **Public Domain**: 100% safe. These sounds have no copyright protection at all. Examples: old government recordings, certain NASA files, very old media.
- **Creative Commons – CC0**: Totally royalty-free and free to use commercially. No attribution required.
- **Creative Commons – Attribution (CC-BY)**: Free to use even commercially, **but you must credit the creator.**
- **Licensed Royalty-Free Libraries**: You can buy or download these from places like [Freesound.org](https://freesound.org), [Zapsplat](https://www.zapsplat.com), or [AudioJungle](https://audiojungle.net) under terms that **explicitly allow commercial use**.

---

#### 🚩 **Gray or Risky Areas**
- **Video Game Rips** (like NES jump sounds, or Tetris rotate clicks): These are copyright-protected. Doesn’t matter if they’re tiny or heavily used online—you could still get a takedown if Nintendo, Sega, etc. feel spicy.
- **Movie/TV sound bites** (“pew pew” from Star Wars, THX sounds): Copyrighted and highly recognizable. Studios *will* strike if money enters the chat.
- **YouTube SFX packs** that don’t list license terms: Assume risky unless they explicitly say CC0 or similar.
- **Remixed/Meme Sounds**: Even pitch-shifted or altered clips can still be flagged if the original source is known.

---

### 💰 **If Icepick Gets Popular or Monetized**
If you're making:
- **Money directly** (sales, ads, donations tied to the game)
- **A name for yourself through the game** (brand recognition, exposure, press)

...then you’re legally expected to **license every asset you don’t fully own.**

Even *one* unlicensed sound can mess you up with a takedown or demonetization.

### 🔧 What You Can Do

- Find a **legit-sourced “soundalike”**, or
