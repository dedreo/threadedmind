Assistant
Yeah, *"losing that liquor weight"* is **absolutely** a real and common thing—so much so that it's not just book talk, it’s something doctors and dietitians see all the time in recovery or lifestyle changes. And with **16 months of sobriety** under your belt? You’re well into the stage where those effects start to really show and stabilize. Especially when you’re pairing that with **GLP-1 meds**, which already help reduce appetite and regulate blood sugar—you're basically compounding progress in a good way.

### Here’s what’s behind “liquor weight”:

1. **Empty Calories**
Alcohol is dense in calories (7 calories per gram) with *zero nutrition*. A few drinks a day can easily add hundreds or even a thousand calories on top of your normal intake.

2. **Sugar & Carbs**
A lot of alcoholic drinks (especially beer, wine, or mixers) are loaded with sugars or fast carbs that get stored as fat—especially **visceral fat** around the belly.

3. **Metabolic Disruption**
Chronic drinking messes with your liver’s fat-processing ability and spikes your cortisol levels, leading to fat storage and bloating.

4. **Bloating & Water Retention**
Alcohol makes the body hold onto fluids in weird ways, causing puffiness, especially in the face, hands, and stomach.

5. **Sleep & Recovery**
Booze wrecks your sleep quality, which in turn messes up your metabolism and makes cravings worse—removing alcohol lets your body start to heal and reset.

---

### The 16-Month Mark:
That’s a **huge** milestone. By this point:
- Your liver is likely far healthier
- Your hormone regulation is improving
- Your gut microbiome is healing
- Inflammation is way down
- Visceral fat is more likely to start dropping

And **pairing that with GLP-1** (which enhances satiety, helps you eat less, and nudges your body to burn fat over time), yeah—you’re probably seeing or about to see **real body composition shifts**.

---

If you feel like your shape or size is starting to change without big diet/exercise shifts, that’s totally valid. It could very well be:
- The delayed benefits of long-term sobriety finally kicking in physically
- GLP-1 working gradually
- Less systemic inflammation and better water balance
- Or all of the above!
