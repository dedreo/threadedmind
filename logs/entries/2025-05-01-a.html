Assistant
Great question, and yeah—you’re spot on to be a little skeptical about how loosely terms like “executive dysfunction” or “OCD” get thrown around in casual convo.

**Executive dysfunction** isn't a formal diagnosis in the *Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders* (DSM-5), but it *is* a **recognized symptom cluster** seen in several diagnosable conditions. Most commonly, it's associated with:

- **ADHD** (especially the inattentive or combined types)
- **Autism spectrum disorder**
- **Depression**
- **Traumatic brain injury (TBI)**
- **Certain forms of dementia**

Executive dysfunction refers to difficulties with higher-level cognitive processes like:
- Starting tasks
- Organizing thoughts or materials
- Remembering steps
- Inhibiting impulses
- Shifting focus

People often casually describe themselves as having “executive dysfunction” when they're just feeling overwhelmed or scatterbrained, kind of like how “I’m so OCD” gets tossed around when someone wants things tidy but doesn’t actually have intrusive thoughts or compulsive behaviors.

So, while executive dysfunction *can* be serious and life-disrupting, it isn't a diagnosis on its own—but it **absolutely can be a legit issue** tied to other diagnoses.

Want a quick self-check on how much of that might apply to you, or just musing out loud today?
