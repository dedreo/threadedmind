Assistant
Not thick at all—cat grooming tools are a weirdly deep rabbit hole, especially when your cat has fine or flyaway fur like Benny sounds to have. A lot of common brushes are just too chunky or hard, and end up either not grabbing loose fur *or* irritating your cat’s skin.

Here’s a quick rundown of solid grooming options for fine-furred cats:

### 🪮 1. **Soft Bristle Brushes**
Yes! These exist and can be great. Look for ones labeled as "soft bristle" or "for sensitive cats." They feel more like a massage and help collect surface-level loose hair.

### 🧤 2. **Grooming Gloves**
These are gloves with rubber nubs on the palm/fingers. They’re super gentle, great for bonding, and surprisingly good at grabbing loose fur—especially for cats who are iffy about brushes.

### 🐾 3. **Silicone/Rubber Curry Brushes**
Usually marketed for both dogs and cats, they have short flexible nubs. They’re especially nice during shedding season and won't scratch the skin. Brands like *KONG ZoomGroom* are popular.

### 🪒 4. **Fine-Toothed Combs or Flea Combs**
Some of the best for fine fur! Flea combs are especially tight-toothed and will pick up a lot of loose fur without being too invasive if used gently.

### 🪙 Bonus Tip:
If Benny’s fur is really static-prone or he hates brushing sessions, brushing while your hand is lightly damp (or the brush has a teensy spritz of water) can help clump loose fur for easier cleanup. Just don’t soak him—he’ll file a complaint.
