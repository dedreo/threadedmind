---

### ⚙️ **What “multi-PSU” really means**
You’re not *doubling* power to one card — you’re **splitting the load** across multiple power supplies.
Example:
- PSU #1 powers the motherboard, CPU, and one GPU.
- PSU #2 powers the remaining GPUs and sometimes fans or storage.

They both share a common ground through the case or chassis, and they can be turned on simultaneously using either:
- a **dual-PSU adapter cable** (sometimes called *Add2PSU*, costs ~$10), or
- a motherboard with dual-ATX headers (rare, usually only on mining/workstation boards).

---

### ⚡ **Why people do it**
1. **Power scaling:**
You can run multiple high-draw GPUs (300–450 W each) without needing a single 1600–2000 W monster PSU.
2. **Efficiency:**
Two mid-range PSUs (say, 850 W Platinum) running at 60–70 % load are *more efficient and cooler* than one 1500 W running near max.
3. **Redundancy:**
If one PSU dies, the other can keep low-draw components powered — you don’t lose everything instantly.
4. **Cost control:**
Sometimes it’s cheaper to add a second 850 W PSU you already own than to buy a brand-new 1600 W unit.

---

### 🧩 **When it’s not worth it**
- For **1–2 GPUs**, it’s overkill — a single 1200–1600 W PSU can handle everything easily.
- Adds a *little* cable clutter and more potential points of failure (two sets of fans, two AC cords, more connections).
- You need to be mindful about **grounding** — mix-matching PSUs without proper common ground can cause boot instability (solved by Add2PSU adapters or quality risers).

---

### 💸 **Cost and efficiency reality check**
| Setup | Typical Cost | Efficiency | Pros | Cons |

- **AOMEI Backupper**
