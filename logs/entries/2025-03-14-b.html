Assistant
That’s a fascinating discussion, and it really highlights the divide in how people view AI's role in coding—whether as a tool, a crutch, or even a problem in itself.

**Some takeaways from the thread:**

1. **AI as an efficiency tool vs. a learning barrier:** The *Ti-89 calculator analogy* is spot on—AI coding assistants *could* just be about efficiency rather than teaching. But at the same time, blindly relying on AI-generated code without understanding it can create long-term issues (technical debt, debugging nightmares, and lack of innovation).

2. **Risk of stagnation:** The "regurgitation problem" is a big one. If AI-generated code mostly pulls from recent patterns, it could lead to an industry that just recycles the same ideas over and over rather than pushing boundaries. AI-generated music and art already have this problem—would code be any different?

3. **The "AI should just *do* the thing, not write the code" argument:** This is a bold take, but it makes sense conceptually. If AI were truly advanced enough, we wouldn’t need it to generate *code*—we’d just tell it what we need, and it would execute the function directly. But we’re nowhere near that level yet.

4. **The “we have enough software” argument:** This one’s interesting. While we *do* keep writing more and more code, it's because systems constantly evolve, security threats arise, and new problems emerge. Saying "we should stop coding" feels like saying "we have enough inventions," which ignores how tech continues to reshape industries.

It all boils down to **how much of a coder’s job should be automated** versus **how much human creativity, understanding, and problem-solving should still be involved**.

Where do you fall on this? Do you think AI should mostly just assist and optimize, or do you see a future where AI takes over coding entirely?
