User 13:20:20
with the feats that come with leveling alchemist, are the thrown bottles considered simple missile throwing weapons?
Assistant 13:20:21
Great question—and it’s a bit of a subtle one!
### 🧪 Alchemist Bombs:
- Alchemist bombs are considered Spell-Like Abilities (SLAs), not weapons, even though they behave a lot like thrown weapons.
- They do not benefit from standard thrown weapon feats (like Point Blank Shot, Precise Shot, or Weapon Focus: Thrown) unless specifically stated otherwise in an enhancement or feat.
- They do benefit from Metamagic feats (like Empower, Maximize, etc.) because they’re spell-like.
- They don’t count as “missile weapons” for things like Throwing Mastery or simple/martial weapon proficiency.
---
### 🧨 Thrown Weapons (like Acid Flasks or Alchemical Fire):
These are actual thrown weapons, governed by:
- Simple weapon proficiency (usually doesn’t matter unless you’re getting bonuses).
- Ranged attack feats like Point Blank Shot, Precise Shot, etc.
So if you're using actual grenade-like weapons (from inventory), they count as simple thrown weapons.
But if you're using your Alchemist bombs, they're SLAs and obey spellcasting rules.
---
### TL;DR:
Alchemist bombs = spell-like abilities, not simple missile weapons.
Thrown items from inventory = simple thrown weapons.
---
If you’re building around bombs, it’s all about Metamagic feats and bomb-enhancing enhancements in your tree.
If you ever pivot to use Thrown Weapon builds (some Alchemists do for fun with dualing Kukris + throwers), then those feats apply.

### Short Answer:
Yes—it can be worth it to take Light (or even Medium) Armor Proficiency on an Elven Alchemist, especially if you're aiming for survivability and willing to give up robes or cloth.
But let’s break it down:
---
### 🧝‍♂️ Elven Arcane Fluidity Racial Perk
You’re probably thinking of:
> 🧬 Elven Arcane Fluidity (racial enhancement):
> “Reduces Arcane Spell Failure by 5% per rank (up to 15%).”
So even if you’re wearing armor that normally causes Arcane Spell Failure (ASF), you can cut it way down or even eliminate it.
- Light Armor (ASF ~10-15%): Can be completely negated with Arcane Fluidity + some gear (e.g., Insignias or Blue Augment gems).
- Medium Armor (ASF ~20-25%): Needs full Arcane Fluidity and a bit more help (like gear or Cannith Crafting).
---
### 🎯 Why You’d Want to Wear Armor:
- Higher AC and PRR than robes.
- Tons of named light armor have extra set bonuses, spellpower boosts, or augment slots.
- Some options (like Feytwist or Schism Shard sets) boost DCs, crit chance, or give defensive procs.
- Alchemists can still cast just fine in armor once you negate ASF.
---
### 🪙 Feats Required:
- Light Armor Proficiency – 1 feat (unless you get it from a splash).
- Medium Armor Proficiency – needs Light first, and costs another feat unless you dip a class like Fighter or take a universal tree that grants it.
So the real trade-off is: Is spending a feat worth that defense boost vs. another metamagic or caster feat?
---
### ✨ Consider If:
- You’re soloing a lot or doing Reaper content.
- You’re not strapped for feats (you’ve got Empower, Maximize, Quicken, etc. already).
- You have access to good light/medium armor that makes it worth equipping (e.g., sets with spellpower, DCs, PRR, HP bonuses).
- You’re investing in Arcane Fluidity anyway.
---
### ❌ Skip If:
- You’re in cloth-focused sets that give better caster bonuses (some robes have +Evoc DCs, +Spell Pen, etc.).
- You’re tight on feats and need every slot for metamagic or casting support.
- You like to dump Arcane Fluidity to invest elsewhere in the Elf tree.

- Light Armor Proficiency on an Elf Alchemist? ✅ Yes, it can be worth it, especially for Reaper/tanky solo play or if you're flush with feats.
