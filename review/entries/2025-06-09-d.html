Assistant 11:12:35
Yep—in most cases, yes, you'd be legally in the clear using an audio-specialized LLM to generate a custom "oh no!" voice line from your prompt, especially for indie or non-commercial use. Here's how it breaks down:
---
### ✅ You're likely safe if:
1. You wrote the prompt
The creative direction is yours, so the output is considered user-generated.
2. The output is original
Most AI audio tools generate new vocal samples, not ripped or copied ones. As long as it doesn't exactly replicate the Lemmings voice or sample a copyrighted line, you're fine.
3. The model doesn't use licensed voices without permission
Some services let you use celebrity or game-character voices (e.g., FakeYou). These are fun, but not legal for public/commercial use. You'd want to stick with generic or AI-only voices, or your own recording.
4. You're not passing off a trademarked sound
The original Lemmings "oh no!" is likely copyrighted as part of the game's assets. A clearly inspired, not-identical version is legally fine—parody, homage, stylization are fair game.
---
### ⚠️ Caution zones:
- If you ask for "the exact Lemmings 'oh no!' voice" and get a near-perfect match, that could be dicey—especially if the game is commercial.
- If you're using a voice model based on real people or IP (e.g., “do it in the voice of [famous actor/game character]”), don’t use it in public or monetized projects.
---
### 🧠 Safer phrasing for prompts:
- ✅ “In the style of a retro cartoon character”
- ✅ “Bitcrushed, high-pitched panic voice for a falling blob”
- ✅ “1-second exclamation, like an old game sprite reacting to doom”
---
### TL;DR:
Yes, you're legally okay using audio generated from your own creative prompt using a non-IP voice, especially if it's original and not impersonating anyone. If you're careful with the phrasing and sources, you're good—even for a commercial indie game.
