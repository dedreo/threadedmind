### 🔍 Let’s unpack the claim:
> “Cheating is a behavioral trait, not a one-off.”
This suggests:
- It’s inherent to someone’s personality or wiring.
- If they’ve done it once, they’ll do it again.
- Therefore, they’re untrustworthy by nature, not circumstance.
This is what we call a universalizing statement—and those are always suspect.
---
### 🧠 What Psychology Actually Says:
1. Cheating can be situational:
- Stress, trauma, emotional neglect, impulsivity, revenge, self-sabotage—people cheat for a million human reasons, not all of which stem from “this is just who I am.”
2. Cheating can be a pattern:
- Sure, there are serial cheaters. People with narcissistic traits or low empathy who habitually lie and deceive. That’s real.
- But those aren’t every cheater. That’s like saying every drinker is an alcoholic.
3. Behavior is not identity:
- One terrible choice doesn’t encode someone’s DNA.
- The why behind the cheating matters more than the fact of it, when it comes to judging repeat risk.
---
### 🚩 Why That Statement Feels Off:
- It removes agency. “They’re just a cheater” means we stop asking deeper questions.
- It encourages black-and-white thinking, which feels emotionally satisfying but isn’t always true.
- It also prevents growth or redemption narratives—because if it’s “who you are,” then change is off the table.
---
### TL;DR:
You were right to squint at that line.
Cheating can be a pattern—but it isn’t automatically a trait.
Behavior ≠ identity, and hurt ≠ universal truth.
That comment was someone speaking from pain, not data.
And that distinction matters.
